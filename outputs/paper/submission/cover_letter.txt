Cover Letter — IEEE Transactions on Medical Imaging

Dear Editor-in-Chief,

We are pleased to submit our manuscript, "FreeReg: Free-Coordinate Registration
of Arbitrary 3D Models to Arbitrary Image Streams for Augmented and Mixed
Reality," for consideration as a Regular Paper in IEEE Transactions on Medical
Imaging.

Augmented-reality guidance in interventional medicine hinges on a registration
problem: aligning a preoperative 3D model with the live endoscopic view. Deployed
systems either depend on external tracking hardware (electromagnetic navigation)
or assume calibrated, rigid, per-frame conditions that fail under uncalibrated
optics, tissue deformation, and temporal jitter. We formulate this as
free-coordinate estimation of the projection mapping among model, camera, and
pixel frames, in which intrinsics, depth, rigid pose, and deformation are each
optional and degradable. FreeReg combines a multi-source correspondence engine
with contour-gated arbitration, an observability-guided non-rigid estimator
confined to the spectrally observable subspace, a manifold SE(3) Kalman filter
with innovation gating, and a co-anchored registration-rendering field whose
photometric residual serves as an online, label-free validation signal.

One and the same engine is validated across rigid CAD (BOP Linemod/T-LESS),
deformable tissue (real-colon FEM), and clinical CT-to-bronchoscopy and SCARED
endoscopy. It matches or exceeds state of the art where such methods apply (BOP
AR 0.848 vs FoundationPose 0.847; T-LESS ADD-S 0.921) and remains correct where
they collapse out-of-distribution: on clinical endoscopy, FoundationPose reaches
104 mm and Endo3R 34 mm error, while FreeReg reaches 4.23 mm mean target
registration error at 30-80 FPS — within the accuracy range of electromagnetic
navigation, without any external tracking hardware. Our observability analysis
further quantifies which deformation directions a given sensing condition can
and cannot recover, and shows that a second view nearly doubles the recoverable
subspace (23% to 41%).

We believe this work is well suited to IEEE TMI. Registration is a recognized
bottleneck in image-guided intervention, and FreeReg addresses it with a
camera-only, calibration-free approach whose accuracy, robustness, and explicit
reliability limits are directly relevant to the clinical adoption of surgical AR
— while its free-coordinate formulation and observability theory are of broad
methodological interest to the medical-imaging community.

This manuscript is original, has not been published previously, and is not under
consideration elsewhere. All authors have read and approved the submission and
declare no conflict of interest. The clinical data are de-identified and used
under the institutional data-governance process of Henan Provincial People's
Hospital (see Ethics Statement). The full pipeline and evaluation scripts are
publicly released at https://github.com/liranyang123456-commits/FreeReg for
reproducibility.
