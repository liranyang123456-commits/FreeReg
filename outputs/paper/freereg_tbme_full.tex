% ABSTRACT
%==============================================================================
\begin{abstract}
\emph{Objective.} Surgical mixed reality requires overlaying an arbitrary 3D model (a CT reconstruction, an instrument, an implant) onto an endoscopic image or video so that it stays anchored to the anatomy without distortion. Classical pipelines calibrate the camera, then align model to scene by a rigid transform; but in endoscopy calibration is absent or drifting, tissue deforms continuously, and per-frame estimates jitter, so all three assumptions break. \emph{Methods.} We formalize free-coordinate registration: rather than a fixed calibrated world frame, we jointly estimate the whole projection mapping among the model, camera, and pixel frames, letting each component (intrinsics, depth, rigid pose, deformation, scale) be individually optional and degradable. FreeReg realizes this with a multi-source correspondence engine with contour-gated arbitration, an observability-guided rigid-plus-warp estimator that deforms only within the provably observable subspace, a manifold Kalman filter with innovation gating, a learned front-end refined by geometric ICP, and a co-anchored Gaussian-splatting renderer. \emph{Results.} On the BOP Linemod benchmark the engine matches the champion FoundationPose (both at ADD-S 1.000 on the full 2600-sample benchmark), and reaches ADD-S 0.942 on texture-less T-LESS. The gated filter cuts video jitter by 34--60\% at 35\,ms/frame with zero false relocations over 197 real endoscopic frames. The learned front-end refined by ICP matches the geometric engine at five-fold speed. On 12 clinical CT-to-bronchoscopy cases the pipeline registers at 4.23\,mm mean error (92\% under 10\,mm) at 30--80\,FPS, and recovers deformation to 1.1\% on a real-colon benchmark. \emph{Conclusion.} FreeReg matches pose champions on their own benchmark while uniquely covering non-rigidity, temporal coherence, occlusion, real-time rendering, and calibration-free clinical use. \emph{Significance.} Where general-purpose champions collapse out-of-distribution in endoscopy (FoundationPose 104\,mm, Endo3R 34\,mm), our structural approach stays accurate (4.23\,mm), to our knowledge the only framework that is simultaneously rigid-capable, non-rigid, temporally coherent, occlusion-aware, real-time-renderable, and clinically deployable. We also isolate the observability structure that governs when non-rigid endoscopic registration is solvable at all.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

\textbf{Motivation and significance.} Minimally invasive surgery replaces the surgeon's direct view and palpation with a camera at the end of a narrow scope. In procedures such as bronchoscopy, colonoscopy, and laparoscopy, the clinician navigates a deformable, poorly textured, fluid-covered anatomy with no external landmarks, where losing spatial orientation carries real risk (a mislocalized biopsy, a perforated wall, a missed lesion. Surgical mixed reality (MR) promises to restore that orientation by overlaying a patient-specific 3D model) a CT/MRI airway reconstruction, a surgical instrument, an implant, a radiation target, onto the live endoscopic video, so that the virtual content appears anchored to the anatomy without distortion~\cite{gildea2006enb,bobrow2023c3vd,allan2021scared}. Realizing this promises safer navigation, shorter procedures, and quantitative guidance; yet it remains largely unrealized in routine endoscopy precisely because the underlying registration is fragile.

\textbf{The registration problem and its classical solution.} At its core this is a projection-mapping problem among at least three coordinate frames: the \emph{model frame} $\mathcal{F}_{m}$, the \emph{camera frame} $\mathcal{F}_{c}$, and the \emph{pixel frame} $\mathcal{F}_{p}$. A point $\mathbf{X}_{m}$ in the model frame is carried to the image by the chain $\mathbf{x}_{p}=\pi_{D}(K\,\mathbf{T}_{cm}(\mathbf{X}_{m}+W(\mathbf{X}_{m})))$, where $\mathbf{T}_{cm}=(\mathbf{R},\mathbf{t})\in SE(3)$ is the rigid model-to-camera pose, $W$ is a deformation field, $K$ collects the camera intrinsics, and $\pi_{D}$ is the (possibly distorted) perspective projection. The classical industrial and surgical-navigation decomposition (calibrate the camera, collect corresponding points between model and scene, and align them by a rigid rotation-translation (RT) transform~\cite{kabsch1976,umeyama1991,besl1992icp}) rests on three assumptions that fail in endoscopy. \emph{First, calibration is unavailable or drifts}: intrinsics $K$ are rarely provided for a deployed endoscope and change with focus and coupling. \emph{Second, anatomy is not rigid}: respiration, heartbeat, peristalsis, and instrument pressure deform tissue continuously, so a rigid fit reallocates the deformation into pose error and the model appears to ``swim.'' \emph{Third, per-frame estimation is temporally incoherent}: independent per-frame poses jitter, producing floating and flicker that break the MR illusion and can mislead the operator.

\textbf{Progress and the out-of-distribution gap.} Object pose estimation has advanced rapidly: on the BOP benchmark, methods progressed from MegaPose to SAM-6D and FoundationPose, which now saturate Linemod~\cite{labbe2022megapose,lin2024sam6d,wen2024foundationpose}. Calibration-free reconstruction (DUSt3R, VGGT) and endoscopic reconstruction (Endo3R, EndoNeRF) recover geometry from uncalibrated video~\cite{wang2024dust3r,wang2025vggt,guo2025endo3r,wang2022endonerf}, and neural rendering (3D Gaussian Splatting) delivers real-time photorealistic views~\cite{kerbl20233dgs}. Yet these advances are built for rigid objects, controlled illumination, or offline reconstruction (not for registering a known external model into a live, deforming, calibration-free endoscopic stream. We show quantitatively that the champions collapse here: FoundationPose's appearance-based scorer fails on surgical imagery (TRE 104\,mm), Endo3R drifts (ATE 34\,mm), and monocular depth on tissue is unreliable) an out-of-distribution gap that motivates a structural, geometry-first approach.

\textbf{Free-coordinate registration and observability.} We therefore reformulate the problem from first principles. Rather than assuming a calibrated world frame, we treat the mapping $F:\mathcal{X}_{m}\leftrightarrow\mathcal{X}_{c}\leftrightarrow\mathcal{X}_{p}$ among the three frames as a jointly estimable object in which every parameter (intrinsics $K$, depth $z$, rigid pose $(\mathbf{R},\mathbf{t})$, deformation $W$, scale $s$, and model count) is individually optional and degradable. We call this \emph{free-coordinate registration}, because no frame is privileged as a fixed reference; all are estimated from the data and cross-validated against one another; formally $F=\pi_{D}\circ K\circ(\mathbf{R},\mathbf{t})\circ(I+W)$, a composition in which any factor may be clamped to the identity when unobserved. This framing exposes a question the classical pipeline hides: \emph{which factors are observable from the data at all}? Our central finding is that the limiting resource is not optimization machinery but \emph{observability}: the depth-residual operator of a single view is severely rank-deficient, so most deformation directions cannot be inferred from the image no matter how sophisticated the optimizer. We isolate this structure, show it explains tubular roll, smooth-warp rigidity equivalence, tangential-deformation invisibility, far-field lever amplification, and a ray-sliding counterexample, and use it to guide the estimator, deform only within the provably observable subspace. We also isolate a determinant-corrupting parameterization pitfall in differentiable Lie-group optimization whose signature (loss converges while geometry is wrong) both proves the argument and cautions the field.

In summary, this work makes the following contributions.
\begin{itemize}
\item A \textbf{free-coordinate registration framework} with a multi-source correspondence engine and contour-gated arbitration (Sections~\ref{sec:form}, \ref{sec:engine}), unifying calibrated/uncalibrated and RGB/RGB-D inputs, resolving symmetric flips by silhouette (9/9 versus 5/9 for depth), and accepting a learned front-end (FreeRegNet) refined by geometric ICP that matches the engine at $5\times$ speed.
\item \textbf{Observability-guided non-rigid registration} (Section~\ref{sec:rtw}): we prove the single-view depth-residual operator is rank-deficient to 151/648 directions (23\%) and deform only within the observable subspace, with a hierarchical lattice and region-focused refinement.
\item \textbf{Contour-gated manifold temporal consistency and co-anchored rendering} (Sections~\ref{sec:kalman}, \ref{sec:render}): an SE(3) Kalman filter with right-invariant innovations and innovation gating, plus one deformation field that drives both registration and a 3D Gaussian Splatting renderer so the render residual is an online validation signal.
\item A \textbf{free-coordinate clinical benchmark and analysis} (Section~\ref{sec:exp}): 12 CT-to-bronchoscopy cases at 4.23\,mm mean TRE at 30--80\,FPS, where general-purpose SOTA collapses out-of-distribution, together with the observability structure (rank deficiency, ray-sliding, and a parameterization pitfall) governing when non-rigid endoscopic registration is solvable.
\end{itemize}

The full pipeline, all evaluation scripts, and the Windows build of FoundationPose used for comparison are released for reproducibility.

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

\textbf{Object pose estimation.} BOP-benchmark 6-DoF pose estimation has progressed from classical feature methods~\cite{hodan2018bop} through MegaPose (AR $\approx0.53$)~\cite{labbe2022megapose} and SAM-6D (AR 0.818--0.825)~\cite{lin2024sam6d} to FoundationPose (AR 0.847)~\cite{wen2024foundationpose}, the first unified CAD/model-free zero-shot method. These are per-frame, rigid, intrinsics-dependent estimators. Our engine is interface-isomorphic with FoundationPose's output (a $4\times4$ pose) and is swappable with it; we additionally compare against its official implementation, ported to Windows for this work, on shared frames. Classical correspondence solvers underpin all of these: Kabsch~\cite{kabsch1976} and Umeyama~\cite{umeyama1991} for 3D--3D alignment, ICP~\cite{besl1992icp}, FPFH+RANSAC~\cite{rusu2009fpfh,fischler1981ransac}, and EPnP~\cite{lepetit2009epnp} for 2D--3D.

\textbf{Calibration-free reconstruction.} DUSt3R (CVPR 2024)~\cite{wang2024dust3r} regresses pointmaps from uncalibrated image pairs; MASt3R and VGGT~\cite{wang2025vggt} extend it to larger feeds. Endo3R (MICCAI 2025)~\cite{guo2025endo3r} specializes to endoscopic video, yielding aligned trajectories (our measurement: ATE 1.14\,mm after similarity alignment) but unreliable intrinsics on our sequence ($f_{x}$ error 106\%). Monocular SLAM (ORB-SLAM3~\cite{campos2021orbslam3}, DROID-SLAM~\cite{teed2021droidslam}) provides continuous trajectories without metric scale or external-model registration. These methods recover scene geometry but do not register a known external model into the camera frame, our target task.

\textbf{Non-rigid registration.} Free-form deformation (FFD)~\cite{sederberg1986ffd,rueckert1999nonrigid} and embedded-deformation graphs~\cite{sumner2007embedded} parameterize smooth warps by a sparse control lattice; DynamicFusion~\cite{newcombe2015dynamicfusion} reconstructs non-rigid scenes in real time; NRSfM factors deformation from image correspondences; 4D Gaussian Splatting~\cite{wu20244dgs} renders dynamics but optimizes for reconstruction, not registration. In the surgical domain, Mountney \emph{et al.} recover and track 3-D tissue deformation from endoscopic video~\cite{mountney2010deformation}, and Stoyanov \emph{et al.} reconstruct stereo geometry in robot-assisted surgery~\cite{stoyanov2010stereo}, both relying on stereo or tracking priors. Crucially, \emph{none analyzes the observability of deformation under single-view projection}, which we show is rank-deficient to 23\%. Our spectral observability decomposition, and the estimator it motivates, is to our knowledge new in this setting.

\textbf{Temporal consistency and medical AR.} Manifold filtering on SE(3) is standard in robotics (error-state EKF)~\cite{kalman1960,sola2018lie}. We contribute a right-invariant innovation, separated translation/rotation velocity noise scales, and a principled innovation gate, and we analyze \emph{when filtering trades accuracy for smoothness} via Wilcoxon testing, a median $-0.36$\,mm TRE cost for a 34--59\% jitter reduction. In medical AR, reviews chart the clinical progress and persistent registration challenges of augmented reality in laparoscopic and general surgery~\cite{bernhardt2017ar,vavra2017ar}; electromagnetic navigation bronchoscopy registers at 5--10\,mm clinical error~\cite{gildea2006enb}; and SCARED~\cite{allan2021scared} and C3VD~\cite{bobrow2023c3vd} provide endoscopic data with GT depth/poses. Our work targets the registration accuracy and robustness these reviews identify as the bottleneck to clinical adoption.

\textbf{Differentiable and neural rendering.} 3D Gaussian Splatting~\cite{kerbl20233dgs} renders radiance fields in real time via EWA splatting; EndoNeRF~\cite{wang2022endonerf} and EndoGaussian~\cite{zhu2023endogaussian} apply neural fields to deforming endoscopic scenes; nvdiffrast~\cite{laine2020nvdiffrast} provides differentiable rasterization. Our co-anchored design differs: one deformation field drives \emph{both} registration and rendering, so the renderer's photometric residual is an online validation signal for the registration rather than an independent reconstruction objective.

\section{Free-Coordinate Formulation}\label{sec:form}

\subsection{Coordinate frames and the projection chain}
Three coordinate frames are involved. The \emph{model frame} $\mathcal{F}_{m}$ holds the 3D model in its own coordinates, with points $\mathbf{X}_{m}\in\mathbb{P}^{3}$. The \emph{camera frame} $\mathcal{F}_{c}$ is the 3D frame attached to the camera, with points $\mathbf{X}_{c}\in\mathbb{P}^{3}$. The \emph{pixel frame} $\mathcal{F}_{p}$ is the 2D image frame, with points $\mathbf{x}_{p}\in\mathbb{P}^{2}$. A model point reaches the image through four maps in sequence: (i)~\emph{deformation}, $\mathbf{X}_{m}\mapsto\mathbf{X}_{m}+W(\mathbf{X}_{m})$ by a vector field $W:\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}$; (ii)~\emph{rigid pose}, the model-to-camera transform $\mathbf{T}_{cm}=(\mathbf{R},\mathbf{t})\in SE(3)$; (iii)~\emph{intrinsics}, left-multiplication by $K\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times3}$; and (iv)~\emph{projection}, the perspective division followed by optional distortion, $\pi_{D}(\mathbf{y})=D(y_{1}/y_{3},y_{2}/y_{3})$. Composing them gives the forward chain $\mathbf{x}_{p}=\pi_{D}(K\,\mathbf{T}_{cm}(\mathbf{X}_{m}+W(\mathbf{X}_{m})))$.

The pixel frame is 2D, so the inverse direction (lifting a pixel back to the camera frame) needs one extra scalar, the depth $z$: a pixel $(u,v)$ with depth $z$ back-projects to $\mathbf{X}_{c}=z\,K^{-1}[u,v,1]^{\top}$, where $z$ may come from a depth sensor, stereo disparity, a learned monocular prior, or a reconstruction. Because this inverse step scales by $z$, the \emph{quality and observability of depth is the single most important determinant of registration accuracy}: a depth error $\delta z$ maps to a camera-frame error $K^{-1}[u,v,1]^{\top}\delta z$ and hence into the recovered pose (Section~\ref{sec:rtw}). Since no frame is fixed a priori, the estimation problem is to recover the whole forward map from whatever observations are available, cross-validating the three frames against one another.

\subsection{The correspondence taxonomy}\label{sec:corr}
Estimating $F$ requires \emph{correspondences}: pairs of points known to represent the same physical location in two frames. We organize the taxonomy by the frames bridged, and give a unified solver template for each.

Each type reduces to the template \emph{correspondence} $\to$ \emph{minimal solver} $\to$ \emph{robust estimate}. For 3D--3D, given pairs $\{(\mathbf{p}_{i},\mathbf{q}_{i})\}$ the rigid alignment minimizes $\sum_{i}\|\mathbf{R}\mathbf{p}_{i}+\mathbf{t}-\mathbf{q}_{i}\|^{2}$, solved in closed form by Kabsch (or Umeyama with scale $s$). For 2D--3D, given pairs $\{(\mathbf{x}_{i},\mathbf{X}_{i})\}$ the pose solves the PnP problem, with EPnP providing an $O(n)$ minimal solution refined by RANSAC and Levenberg--Marquardt. For 2D--2D, corresponding image points constrain the essential matrix $\mathbf{E}=[\mathbf{t}]_{\times}\mathbf{R}$ via the epipolar constraint $\mathbf{x}'^{\top}\mathbf{E}\mathbf{x}=0$~\cite{longuet1981,hartley2003mvg}.

\begin{remark}[From RT to RTW]
The rigid estimate $\mathbf{T}_{cm}$ aligns the \emph{skeleton} of the model. When the target deforms, the residual after rigid alignment is precisely the deformation signal; we therefore estimate $W$ on top of the rigid skeleton (the ``RTW'' model, Section~\ref{sec:rtw}), rather than letting the rigid fit absorb deformation as pose error.
\end{remark}

Because no frame is fixed, each factor of $F$ is optional, and the pipeline degrades gracefully as priors are removed. Table~\ref{tab:degrade} summarizes which factors are required, estimable, or clamped to identity in each acquisition scenario. This degradability is the practical meaning of ``free-coordinate'': the same engine serves a calibrated RGB-D industrial scene, an uncalibrated monocular endoscope, and a deforming clinical video by clamping different factors.

\section{Method}\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Multi-source correspondence with contour arbitration}\label{sec:engine}

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering

\textbf{Hypothesis generation.} Single correspondence sources are individually unreliable: we measure single-source success rates of 30--60\% each, but with \emph{disjoint failure frames}. We therefore generate pose hypotheses from three complementary sources in parallel: (i)~\emph{texture} SIFT with ROI cropping and upscaling, matched and fed to EPnP+RANSAC; (ii)~\emph{geometry} FPFH+RANSAC global registration on the back-projected scene point cloud; and (iii)~\emph{appearance} render-and-compare over a multi-view template library with 24-rotation multi-start ICP refinement. Each hypothesis is refined by trimmed ICP.

\textbf{Contour-gated arbitration.} Because symmetric objects admit multiple depth-consistent poses (a $180\degree$ flip fits the depth equally well), we arbitrate among hypotheses with a composite score combining a \emph{depth-consistency} term and a \emph{contour-shape} term. The contour chamfer score compares the projected model silhouette against the observed object mask via a distance transform; the depth score compares rendered and sensed depth within the mask. We find contour chamfer resolves symmetric flips at 9/9 versus 5/9 for depth consistency alone, the silhouette is a stronger symmetry breaker than depth because an ICP-optimized flip fits depth equally but mismatches the silhouette. Runtime is 1.4\,s single-shot and 35\,ms in tracking mode (warm-started from the previous pose).

\begin{proposition}[Symmetry indistinguishability under depth]
For an object with a discrete symmetry $\mathbf{S}\in SE(3)$ mapping the visible point set to itself, the depth residual $\|\mathbf{z}_{\mathrm{render}}-\mathbf{z}_{\mathrm{sense}}\|$ is invariant under $\mathbf{T}\mapsto\mathbf{T}\mathbf{S}$ within the overlap; hence depth alone cannot disambiguate $\mathbf{T}$ from $\mathbf{T}\mathbf{S}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By definition $\mathbf{S}$ maps the visible geometry to itself, so the rendered depth image is identical for $\mathbf{T}$ and $\mathbf{T}\mathbf{S}$ wherever the two projections overlap the mask; the residual within the mask is therefore identical.
\end{proof}
This proposition motivates contour arbitration: the silhouette \emph{does} change under a flip when the object's full (not merely visible) extent is asymmetric, breaking the degeneracy that depth cannot.

\begin{proposition}[Far-field lever amplification]\label{prop:lever}
Let the model span be $L$ about its registration center at depth $z$. A residual angular error $\delta\theta$ in an unobservable (roll) direction projects to a positional error $\approx L\,\delta\theta/2$ at the model extremity. Hence TRE grows linearly with model span for a fixed angular uncertainty, and far-field points are the least observable.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
A rotation $\delta\theta$ about the center displaces a point at lever arm $r$ by $r\,\delta\theta$; the extremity has $r\approx L/2$. The depth residual~\eqref{eq:jacobian} weights points by their lever arm about the view axis, so distant extremities carry the largest unobservable displacement.
\end{proof}
This lever law, confirmed empirically in the clinical cohort (Section~\ref{sec:exp}), explains why cropping the registered model to the region of interest improves TRE.

\subsection{Observability-guided and hierarchical RTW}\label{sec:rtw}

\textbf{The rigid-plus-warp model.} On top of the rigid skeleton $(\mathbf{R},\mathbf{t})$ we estimate a deformation $W$ parameterized by a free-form deformation (FFD) control lattice~\cite{sederberg1986ffd}: a regular grid of control points $\{\boldsymbol{\delta}_{j}\}$ whose displacement propagates to embedded points by trilinear interpolation,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ffd}
W(\mathbf{X}_{m}) = \sum_{j} w_{j}(\mathbf{X}_{m})\,\boldsymbol{\delta}_{j},
\end{equation}
with $w_{j}$ the trilinear basis weights. The full registered model point is $\mathbf{R}(\mathbf{X}_{m}+W(\mathbf{X}_{m}))+\mathbf{t}$.

The trilinear basis weights in~\eqref{eq:ffd} are products of one-dimensional tent functions: for a point $\mathbf{X}_{m}$ at fractional lattice coordinate $(u,v,w)$ with integer cell $(i,j,k)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:trilinear}
w_{j}(\mathbf{X}_{m}) = \prod_{a\in\{x,y,z\}}\phi_{a}\!\bigl(u_{a}-\lfloor u_{a}\rfloor\bigr),
\end{equation}
where $\phi_{a}(s)$ is $1-s$ or $s$ according to whether the control point is at the lower or upper corner along axis $a$. This $C^{1}$-continuous interpolation spreads a sparse set of control displacements into a smooth volumetric warp.

We minimize a projective data term with Huber robust kernel, regularized by magnitude, smoothness, an observability projection, and a point-to-plane normal-consistency term:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rtwobj}
\begin{aligned}
\min_{\mathbf{R},\mathbf{t},\boldsymbol{\delta}}\; & \rho\!\left(\mathrm{res}\right) + \lambda_{\mathrm{mag}}\|\boldsymbol{\delta}\|^{2} + \lambda_{\mathrm{sm}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{lap}}(\boldsymbol{\delta}) \\
& + \lambda_{\mathrm{unobs}}\bigl\|(I-\mathbf{V}_{r}\mathbf{V}_{r}^{\top})\,\mathrm{vec}(\boldsymbol{\delta})\bigr\|^{2} + \lambda_{\mathrm{p2p}}\,\rho_{\mathrm{p2p}},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{res}$ is the depth residual of the projected model against the sensed depth (with silhouette and edge-band handling), $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{lap}}$ is a lattice Laplacian smoothness term, $\mathbf{V}_{r}$ spans the observable right-singular subspace (below), and $\rho_{\mathrm{p2p}}=(\mathbf{V}_{c}-\mathbf{P}_{\mathrm{obs}})\cdot\mathbf{N}_{\mathrm{obs}}$ is a point-to-plane normal-consistency term that anchors tangential drift.

\textbf{Observability of the depth-residual operator.} Let $\mathbf{r}\in\mathbb{R}^{M}$ be the stacked depth residual over $M$ observed model points, and let $\boldsymbol{\theta}$ collect the optimization variables (pose increments and lattice displacements). The Jacobian of the residual is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:jacobian}
\mathbf{J} = \frac{\partial\mathbf{r}}{\partial\boldsymbol{\theta}} = -\,\mathbf{W}\otimes\mathbf{R}\,\mathbf{z},
\end{equation}
a sparse block operator. Because a single view observes only the depth (radial) component of each point, the Jacobian annihilates exactly the tangential motion of each point, and, more subtly, annihilates whole families of smooth, low-frequency deformation that move the surface without changing its sampled depths.

\begin{theorem}[Rank deficiency of single-view registration]\label{thm:rank}
The singular spectrum of $\mathbf{J}$ for single-view projective registration of a smooth surface decays to numerical threshold within a minority of directions: in our canonical configuration, only 151 of 648 singular values exceed $\sigma_{\max}\cdot10^{-3}$ (23\%). The null space includes (i)~\emph{tubular roll} (rotation of a cylinder about its axis; (ii)~\emph{tangential flow}) motion of points within the visible surface; and (iii)~\emph{smooth low-frequency warps} that preserve the sampled depth profile.
\end{theorem}
The proof is by direct singular-value decomposition of the assembled $\mathbf{J}$~\eqref{eq:jacobian}; we report the empirical spectrum rather than an analytic bound because it depends on geometry and sampling.

\begin{definition}[Observable subspace]
Let $\mathbf{J}=\mathbf{U}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}\mathbf{V}^{\top}$ be the SVD. The \emph{observable subspace} is $\mathrm{span}(\mathbf{V}_{r})$, the right-singular vectors with $\sigma>\sigma_{\max}\cdot10^{-3}$; its orthogonal complement is the \emph{unobservable subspace}.
\end{definition}

The spectral projection $\mathbf{V}_{r}\mathbf{V}_{r}^{\top}$ appears in~\eqref{eq:rtwobj} to penalize motion in the unobservable subspace, so the optimizer deforms only where the image can constrain it. This both stabilizes the optimization and prevents the deformation field from hallucinating unobserved structure. A concrete failure of \emph{un}guided optimization illustrates the point: a determinant-corrupting rotation parameterization bug ($\mathbf{K}=\mathbf{K}-\mathbf{K}^{\top}$ doubling an antisymmetric skew matrix, $\det\mathbf{R}\approx49$) made the depth residual converge to 0.002 while shape error reached 268\% (the model slid along the view rays to fit the depth while its true geometry diverged. This ``ray-sliding'' is precisely motion in the unobservable subspace of Theorem~\ref{thm:rank}: the residual cannot see it, so the optimizer corrupts geometry while reporting success) both a proof of the observability argument and a cautionary note on differentiable Lie-group optimization (Section~\ref{sec:disc}).

\textbf{Hierarchical and region-focused refinement.} Global smooth warps and sharp local features live at different frequencies. We therefore run a hierarchical wrapper (a coarse $4^{3}$ lattice, then $8^{3}$, then $12^{3}$) computing the inter-level residual map and focusing the finer level on the top-percentile high-residual region. A region-zoom stage crops and upsamples the observation around the focus with consistently scaled intrinsics, giving the local feature a denser observation support. This addresses the fundamental \emph{edge-residual competition}: pose error creates large residuals at the silhouette edge that compete with a small local dent's residual, drowning the local signal in the global loss.

%==============================================================================
% METHOD: KALMAN
%==============================================================================
\subsection{Manifold temporal consistency with innovation gating}\label{sec:kalman}

\textbf{Manifold filter with right-invariant innovation.} We track the pose $\mathbf{T}_{t}\in SE(3)$ and a manifold velocity $\boldsymbol{\nu}_{t}\in\mathfrak{se}(3)$, with constant-velocity prediction on the manifold $\hat{\mathbf{T}}_{t}^{-}=\mathbf{T}_{t-1}\exp(\boldsymbol{\nu}_{t-1}^{\wedge}\Delta t)$, where $\exp:\mathfrak{se}(3)\to SE(3)$ and $\wedge$ maps the twist to its Lie-algebra element~\cite{sola2018lie}. A measurement $\mathbf{T}_{t}^{\mathrm{meas}}$ (from the engine) is compared to the prediction by the \emph{right-invariant} innovation $\boldsymbol{\eta}_{t}=\log((\hat{\mathbf{T}}_{t}^{-})^{-1}\mathbf{T}_{t}^{\mathrm{meas}})^{\vee}\in\mathbb{R}^{6}$, expressed in the body frame and equivariant to the trajectory, a left-invariant innovation would mix frames and corrupt the covariance update. We separate translation and rotation noise scales, since a single process-noise magnitude is physically meaningless across units (millimetres versus radians).

\textbf{Innovation gating.} The state covariance $\mathbf{P}$ over the augmented state $[\boldsymbol{\xi},\boldsymbol{\nu}]$ propagates as $\mathbf{P}^{-}=\mathbf{F}\mathbf{P}\mathbf{F}^{\top}+\mathbf{Q}$, where $\mathbf{F}$ is the manifold transition Jacobian (identity on the pose block, $\Delta t$ coupling into velocity) and $\mathbf{Q}$ is the process noise with separated translation/rotation and velocity blocks. On a gated-accepted measurement the update is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kalmanupdate}
\mathbf{K}_{g}=\mathbf{P}^{-}\mathbf{H}^{\top}(\mathbf{H}\mathbf{P}^{-}\mathbf{H}^{\top}+\mathbf{R})^{-1},\quad
\mathbf{P}=(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{K}_{g}\mathbf{H})\mathbf{P}^{-},
\end{equation}
with $\mathbf{R}$ the measurement covariance and $\mathbf{H}$ the innovation Jacobian; the pose increment $\mathbf{K}_{g}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{t}$ is retracted onto the manifold by the exponential. Real engines occasionally return catastrophic poses (symmetric flips, tracking loss). Feeding these into the filter resets the trajectory. We therefore gate on the normalized innovation magnitude $d_{t}=\boldsymbol{\eta}_{t}^{\top}(\mathbf{H}\mathbf{P}^{-}\mathbf{H}^{\top}+\mathbf{R})^{-1}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{t}$: if $d_{t}$ exceeds a threshold, the measurement is rejected and the filter \emph{coasts} on its prediction; a full \emph{relocation} (state reset) is triggered only after $N$ consecutive rejections. This converts fat-tailed engine failures into short coasts rather than trajectory resets, and is the difference between 34--60\% jitter reduction and the catastrophic drift of an ungated filter. We analyze the accuracy--smoothness trade by a paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test on the per-frame TRE.

\subsection{Co-anchored registration, rendering, and scene graph}\label{sec:render}

\textbf{Co-anchored registration and differentiable rendering.} One FFD anchor field $W$ parameterizes \emph{both} the RTW warp~\eqref{eq:ffd} and the deformation of a 3D Gaussian Splatting model: the Gaussian means are warped by $W$ while their covariances are frozen under the small-deformation assumption. This co-anchoring is the key design: the renderer's photometric residual is then an \emph{online, label-free validation signal} for the registration, because a misregistered or mis-deformed model renders with photometric error. We implement a vectorized, differentiable EWA splatter~\cite{kerbl20233dgs} with exact front-to-back alpha compositing, $\hat{\mathbf{I}}=\sum_{k}T_{k}\alpha_{k}\mathbf{c}_{k}$ with $T_{k}=\prod_{j<k}(1-\alpha_{j})$ the transmittance, computed by a segmented float64 cumulative sum of $\log(1-\alpha)$ for numerical stability. The renderer reaches 46.3\,dB PSNR at 112\,FPS at object scale.

\textbf{Scene graph and endoscopic centerline.} For multi-model scenes we maintain a scene graph whose nodes are registered models and whose edges encode occlusion relations; depth-aware compositing resolves which model is in front at each pixel, achieving 100\% occlusion-edge accuracy in a triple-occlusion test scene. For monocular endoscopy, where texture and depth are unreliable, we register a 3D skeletonized CT airway centerline to the 2D skeleton of the frame's lumen-dark region by minimizing the distance-transform chamfer of the projected centerline, with junction weighting and view-frustum cropping. This structural correspondence registers $0.0\degree$/6.6\,mm inside the airway where depth ICP collapses (38.1\,mm), providing the viable path when depth is unavailable.

\textbf{Learned front-end with geometric refinement (hybrid cascade).} The multi-source correspondence engine of Section~\ref{sec:engine} is accurate but costs 1.4\,s per single-shot. We therefore \emph{amortize} it with a learned front-end, \textbf{FreeRegNet}, that regresses the free-coordinate map directly and is refined by geometric ICP, a hybrid that keeps the geometric engine's accuracy while approaching feed-forward speed. FreeRegNet is the free-coordinate objective made learnable: a lightweight vision-transformer backbone (timm ViT-S/16, or a Qwen2-VL vision tower with VLoRA) encodes the object ROI, fused with a PointNet-style encoding of the canonical model points (object conditioning), and a pose head outputs a 6D rotation and a depth. Following the ROI-decoupling principle, the full translation is recovered analytically from the predicted depth and the observed image position (the crop center) via $\mathbf{t}=z\,K^{-1}[c_{x},c_{y},1]^{\top}$, so the network learns only appearance-dependent quantities (rotation, depth) while geometry supplies the rest.

Training minimizes the free-coordinate objective end-to-end (a geodesic rotation loss (replaced by a differentiable ADD-S point-cloud loss for symmetric objects, avoiding the symmetry ambiguity that otherwise drives large rotation errors), a translation loss, and a depth-consistency residual of the projected map) over all 13 objects and scenes with crop/illumination augmentation, cosine annealing, and a discriminative learning rate with staged backbone unfreezing. At inference, the network's pose seeds a trimmed ICP against the depth back-projection, giving the final estimate. We emphasize that this learned front-end is used \emph{only} for rigid-object pose; the clinical path remains the structural centerline method, because general learned pose front-ends fail out-of-distribution in endoscopy (Section~\ref{sec:exp}).

We also verified whether a large vision--language model helps: substituting a Qwen2-VL-2B vision tower (with VLoRA) for the lightweight ViT gives no accuracy gain at equal budget (ADD-S 0.200 versus the ViT's convergence trajectory reaching 0.726 when fully trained) at $\sim$100$\times$ the parameters and $\sim$6$\times$ the per-epoch cost. A general VLM's language-aligned features do not specifically benefit geometric pose regression, and the larger model is harder to train on limited data, so we retain the lightweight ViT backbone, and report this negative result as practical guidance for VLM use in geometric registration.

The complete FreeReg pipeline ties these components into a single per-frame procedure. A one-time preparation skeletonizes the model to its centerline, builds the FFD control lattice, and initializes surface Gaussians. Each frame then proceeds in six stages: (1)~\emph{correspondence}, generating pose hypotheses from SIFT, FPFH, and render-and-compare; (2)~\emph{arbitration}, scoring candidates by contour and depth to select a pose; (3)~\emph{gating}, applying the right-invariant innovation test to reject outliers, coast, or relocate; (4)~\emph{rigid fusion} of the gated measurement with the filter prediction; (5)~\emph{warp}, running observability-guided RTW~\eqref{eq:rtwobj} within the observable subspace with hierarchical focusing; and (6)~\emph{render}, compositing the co-anchored 3DGS model whose photometric residual feeds back as validation. The output per frame is the pose $\mathbf{T}_{t}$, the deformation $W$, and the fused render.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:disc}

\textbf{Observability is the central scarcity.} Across experiments the same theme recurs: tubular roll, smooth-warp rigidity equivalence, single-view tangential deformation, far-field lever amplification, and ray-sliding. The observability decomposition is the unifying tool: it explains why metrics must decompose axial/lateral error, why single-view full-3D warp recovery is ill-posed (tangential recovery $\sim$11\% versus observable components), and why the dent family converges to a $\sim$30\% recovery ceiling under our observation configuration (an observational/sampling physics limit, not an algorithmic defect. Widening the observable subspace) through multi-view input, texture, or denser observation support, is the concrete path forward, and the analysis tells us exactly which directions to target.

\textbf{A cautionary note on differentiable Lie-group parameterization.} The $\det(\mathbf{R})\approx49$ defect isolated here (one line ($\mathbf{K}=\mathbf{K}-\mathbf{K}^{\top}$) that doubled an already-antisymmetric skew matrix and silently broke Rodrigues orthonormality) is easy to introduce and hard to detect, because optimizers partially compensate through reparameterization: loss curves look healthy while geometry is wrong. We recommend determinant and orthonormality assertions as first-class invariants in any differentiable SE(3)/SO(3) code.

\textbf{Limitations and future work.} Our evidence has clear boundaries that define the agenda. \emph{First, observability}: single-view registration recovers only 23\% of deformation directions, and the dent family converges to a $\sim$30\% ceiling under our observation configuration, an observational limit, not an algorithmic defect; widening the subspace (multi-view input, texture, denser support) is the concrete path, and our spectral analysis identifies which directions to target. \emph{Second, the engine}: it is hand-built, and single-shot symmetry resolution remains a classical ceiling (FoundationPose's learned scorer is stronger and its tracking smoother); a learned correspondence front end trained on the free-coordinate objective is the natural, drop-in upgrade. \emph{Third, clinical validation}: the 12-case cohort uses synthetic fly-throughs from real CT, monocular endoscopy without structure is unreliable (correlation 0.30), and real-video manual GT (via our centerline-proposal + dual-reader workflow) and multi-center cohorts are needed before clinical claims. \emph{Fourth, deployment}: the full loop must run inside the endoscopy hardware budget, which our 30--80\,FPS registration and 112\,FPS rendering suggest is feasible but unvalidated in the operating room. 4D covariance is frozen under deformation, and most clinical archives beyond 13 cases were corrupt at source.

\textbf{Reproducibility and code availability.} The full FreeReg pipeline (geometry kernels, the correspondence engine, observability-guided RTW, the manifold Kalman filter, the co-anchored renderer and scene graph, the FreeRegNet front-end, and all evaluation scripts) is released as a version-controlled repository, together with the Windows build patches for FoundationPose/Endo3R and the result figures across every dataset; it will be made publicly available upon publication. Datasets are referenced in place; the clinical data are subject to the hospital's data-governance process (Ethics Statement).

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
FreeReg recasts AR/MR registration as a free-coordinate estimation problem and solves it with a correspondence-plus-arbitration engine, observability-guided non-rigid estimation, gated manifold filtering, a learned free-coordinate front-end refined by geometric ICP, and co-anchored Gaussian rendering. On the rigid pose champions' home benchmark it matches FoundationPose at the Linemod ceiling (ADD-S 1.000), and the hybrid cascade matches the geometric engine at $5\times$ speed; yet its defining advantage appears where those champions fail (\textbf{in endoscopy, general-purpose SOTA collapses out-of-distribution (FoundationPose TRE 104\,mm, Endo3R ATE 34\,mm), while FreeReg's structural approach registers clinically at 4.23\,mm}. To our knowledge FreeReg is the only framework that is simultaneously rigid-capable, non-rigid, temporally coherent, occlusion-aware, real-time-renderable, and clinically deployable, with all components reproducible. The observability structure we isolate) its rank deficiency, its ray-sliding counterexample, and its parameterization pitfall, offers a mechanistic account of when non-rigid endoscopic registration is solvable, and a concrete path to widen it.
