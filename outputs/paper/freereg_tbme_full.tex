% ABSTRACT
%==============================================================================
\begin{abstract}
Augmented and mixed reality (AR/MR) require registering an arbitrary 3D model, given in its own coordinates, onto an arbitrary image or video stream---estimating the projection mapping that carries the model into the camera and pixel frames, then compositing a temporally stable, depth-ordered overlay. Classical calibrate-then-align pipelines assume a calibrated camera, a rigid target, and independent per-frame poses---assumptions that fail under uncalibrated optics, deformation, and temporal jitter. We formulate this as free-coordinate estimation of the projection mapping among model, camera, and pixel frames, with intrinsics, depth, rigid pose, and deformation each optional and degradable and no frame privileged as a fixed reference. FreeReg combines a multi-source correspondence engine with contour-gated arbitration, an observability-guided rigid-plus-warp estimator that deforms only within the spectrally observable subspace of the single-view depth-residual operator, a manifold SE(3) Kalman filter with innovation gating, and a co-anchored registration--rendering field whose photometric residual provides online, label-free validation. One and the same engine is validated across five domains with synthetic ground truth: rigid CAD (BOP Linemod/T-LESS), deformable tissue (real-colon FEM), and clinical CT-to-bronchoscopy and SCARED endoscopy. It matches or exceeds SOTA where they apply (BOP-protocol AR 0.848 versus FoundationPose's 0.847; T-LESS ADD-S 0.921) and remains correct where they collapse out-of-distribution---on clinical endoscopy FoundationPose reaches 104\,mm and Endo3R 34\,mm, while FreeReg reaches 4.23\,mm mean target registration error (92\% under 10\,mm) at 30--80\,FPS, matching electromagnetic-navigation accuracy without external tracking hardware; deformation recovery reaches 1.1\% of model extent on real-colon. The observability analysis quantifies which factors a sensing condition can and cannot recover---and shows that adding a second view nearly doubles the observable subspace (23\% to 41\%)---giving explicit reliability limits and a hardware-level upgrade path for AR/MR deployment.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

\textbf{Motivation and significance.} Augmented and mixed reality (AR/MR) promise to overlay digital 3D content---a CAD model, an anatomical reconstruction, a surgical plan---onto a live view of the physical world. Realizing this requires a registration step: estimating the projection mapping that carries a model from its own coordinates into the camera and pixel frames of an arbitrary image or video stream, so the overlay is anchored and depth-ordered. The problem is pervasive---in surgery a preoperative CT airway is aligned to the bronchoscope view to guide biopsy, in industry a CAD part is anchored to a camera feed, in robotics a model is tracked through an uncalibrated stream. Yet registration is rarely well-posed: the camera may be uncalibrated, the target may deform, and the stream may be monocular and jittery. We target this general problem---registering an arbitrary 3D model to an arbitrary image stream for AR/MR---and validate one and the same engine across domains, using image-guided bronchoscopy as a demanding clinical proving ground where deployed electromagnetic navigation still reports 5--10\,mm error~\cite{gildea2006enb} and requires an external field generator.

\textbf{The registration problem and its classical solution.} At its core, model-to-stream registration is a projection-mapping problem among at least three coordinate frames: the \emph{model frame} $\mathcal{F}_{m}$ (the 3D model in its native coordinates---a CT airway, a CAD part, an anatomical target), the \emph{camera frame} $\mathcal{F}_{c}$ (the sensor), and the \emph{pixel frame} $\mathcal{F}_{p}$. A point $\mathbf{X}_{m}$ in the model frame is carried to the image by the chain $\mathbf{x}_{p}=\pi_{D}(K\,\mathbf{T}_{cm}(\mathbf{X}_{m}+W(\mathbf{X}_{m})))$, where $\mathbf{T}_{cm}=(\mathbf{R},\mathbf{t})\in SE(3)$ is the rigid model-to-camera pose, $W$ is a deformation field, $K$ collects the camera intrinsics, and $\pi_{D}$ is the (possibly distorted) perspective projection. The classical decomposition (calibrate the camera, collect corresponding points between model and scene, and align them by a rigid rotation-translation (RT) transform~\cite{kabsch1976,umeyama1991,besl1992icp}) rests on three assumptions that fail in real deployments. \emph{First, calibration is unavailable or drifts}: intrinsics $K$ are rarely provided for a deployed camera and drift with focus and coupling. \emph{Second, the target is not always rigid}: tissue deforms under respiration and instrument pressure, and articulated or compliant objects violate a rigid fit, which reallocates the deformation into pose error so the overlay appears to ``swim.'' \emph{Third, per-frame estimation is temporally incoherent}: independent per-frame poses jitter, producing floating and flicker that break the AR illusion and can mislead the operator.

\textbf{Why general-purpose methods fail on arbitrary streams.} Object pose estimation has advanced rapidly: on the BOP benchmark, methods progressed from MegaPose to SAM-6D and FoundationPose, which now saturate Linemod~\cite{labbe2022megapose,lin2024sam6d,wen2024foundationpose}. Calibration-free reconstruction (DUSt3R, VGGT) and endoscopic reconstruction (Endo3R, EndoNeRF) recover geometry from uncalibrated video~\cite{wang2024dust3r,wang2025vggt,guo2025endo3r,wang2022endonerf}, and neural rendering (3D Gaussian Splatting) delivers real-time photorealistic views~\cite{kerbl20233dgs}. Yet each addresses a narrower setting: rigid objects with calibrated intrinsics (pose estimation), uncontrolled scenes without a known external model (reconstruction), or offline appearance capture (neural rendering). None solves the general problem of registering a \emph{known, arbitrary} model into a \emph{live, deforming, calibration-free} stream. The difficulty is starkest in endoscopy, where we show quantitatively that they collapse: FoundationPose's appearance-based scorer fails on surgical imagery (TRE 104\,mm), Endo3R drifts (ATE 34\,mm), and monocular depth on featureless mucosa is unreliable. The gap is structural, not incremental, motivating a geometry-first method built for the general setting.

\textbf{Free-coordinate registration and observability.} The general task is model-to-image/video AR/MR registration: given a 3D model in its native coordinates and an image or video stream, estimate an explicit mapping into camera and pixel coordinates, then render a temporally stable, depth-ordered model composite. This is coordinate-system registration, not unconstrained visual overlay. Rather than assuming a calibrated world frame, we treat the mapping $F:\mathcal{X}_{m}\leftrightarrow\mathcal{X}_{c}\leftrightarrow\mathcal{X}_{p}$ as jointly estimable, with intrinsics $K$, depth $z$, rigid pose $(\mathbf{R},\mathbf{t})$, deformation $W$, and scale each optional and degradable. We call this \emph{free-coordinate registration}, because no frame is privileged as a fixed reference; factors are estimated from available evidence or clamped when unobserved: $F=\pi_{D}\circ K\circ(\mathbf{R},\mathbf{t})\circ(I+W)$. This framing exposes the central limit: \emph{which factors are observable from the data at all}? The single-view depth-residual operator is severely rank-deficient, so most deformation directions cannot be inferred regardless of optimizer. We isolate this structure and use it to constrain estimation to the observable subspace (Section~\ref{sec:rtw}).

In summary, this work makes the following contributions.
\begin{itemize}
\item A \textbf{free-coordinate AR/MR registration framework} (Sections~\ref{sec:form}, \ref{sec:engine}) mapping an arbitrary 3D model into the camera/pixel frames of an arbitrary image stream for anchored, occlusion-aware display, unifying calibrated/uncalibrated RGB/RGB-D/monocular inputs with each factor optional and degradable---via a multi-source correspondence engine with contour-gated arbitration (9/9 versus 5/9 for depth on symmetric ambiguities) and a learned front-end at $5\times$ speed.
\item \textbf{Observability-guided non-rigid registration} (Section~\ref{sec:rtw}): the single-view depth-residual operator is rank-deficient to 151/648 directions (23\%); we deform only within the observable subspace, giving the first explicit account of which deformations a sensing condition can and cannot recover.
\item \textbf{Gated manifold temporal consistency and co-anchored rendering} (Sections~\ref{sec:kalman}, \ref{sec:render}): an SE(3) Kalman filter with innovation gating, plus one deformation field driving both registration and a Gaussian-splatting renderer whose photometric residual is an online validation signal.
\item \textbf{Cross-domain validation against SOTA} (Section~\ref{sec:exp}): one engine on rigid CAD (BOP Linemod AR 0.848 versus FoundationPose's 0.847; T-LESS ADD-S 0.921), deformable tissue (real-colon, 1.1\% of extent), and clinical CT-to-bronchoscopy (4.23\,mm mean TRE, 92\% under 10\,mm, at 30--80\,FPS---matching electromagnetic-navigation accuracy without external tracking) where general-purpose SOTA collapses (FoundationPose 104\,mm, Endo3R 34\,mm).
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

\textbf{Object pose estimation.} BOP-benchmark 6-DoF pose estimation has progressed from classical feature methods~\cite{hodan2018bop} through MegaPose (AR $\approx0.53$)~\cite{labbe2022megapose} and SAM-6D (AR 0.818--0.825)~\cite{lin2024sam6d} to FoundationPose (AR 0.847)~\cite{wen2024foundationpose}, the first unified CAD/model-free zero-shot method. These are per-frame, rigid, intrinsics-dependent estimators. Our engine is interface-isomorphic with FoundationPose's output (a $4\times4$ pose) and is swappable with it; we additionally compare against its official implementation, ported to Windows for this work, on shared frames. Classical correspondence solvers underpin all of these: Kabsch~\cite{kabsch1976} and Umeyama~\cite{umeyama1991} for 3D--3D alignment, ICP~\cite{besl1992icp}, FPFH+RANSAC~\cite{rusu2009fpfh,fischler1981ransac}, and EPnP~\cite{lepetit2009epnp} for 2D--3D.

\textbf{Calibration-free reconstruction.} DUSt3R (CVPR 2024)~\cite{wang2024dust3r} regresses pointmaps from uncalibrated image pairs; VGGT~\cite{wang2025vggt} extends it to larger feeds. Endo3R (MICCAI 2025)~\cite{guo2025endo3r} specializes to endoscopic video but yields unreliable intrinsics on our sequences. Monocular SLAM (ORB-SLAM3~\cite{campos2021orbslam3}, DROID-SLAM~\cite{teed2021droidslam}) provides trajectories without metric scale or external-model registration. These recover scene geometry but do not register a known external model into the camera frame, our target task.

\textbf{Non-rigid registration.} Free-form deformation (FFD)~\cite{sederberg1986ffd,rueckert1999nonrigid} and embedded-deformation graphs~\cite{sumner2007embedded} parameterize smooth warps by a sparse control lattice; DynamicFusion~\cite{newcombe2015dynamicfusion} reconstructs non-rigid scenes in real time; NRSfM factors deformation from image correspondences; 4D Gaussian Splatting~\cite{wu20244dgs} renders dynamics but optimizes for reconstruction, not registration. In the surgical domain, Mountney \emph{et al.} recover and track 3-D tissue deformation from endoscopic video~\cite{mountney2010deformation}, and Stoyanov \emph{et al.} reconstruct stereo geometry in robot-assisted surgery~\cite{stoyanov2010stereo}, both relying on stereo or tracking priors. Crucially, \emph{none analyzes the observability of deformation under single-view projection}, which we show is rank-deficient to 23\%. Our spectral observability decomposition, and the estimator it motivates, is to our knowledge new in this setting.

\textbf{Temporal consistency and medical AR.} Manifold filtering on SE(3) is standard in robotics (error-state EKF)~\cite{kalman1960,sola2018lie}. We contribute a right-invariant innovation, separated translation/rotation velocity noise scales, and a principled innovation gate, and we analyze \emph{when filtering trades accuracy for smoothness}. In medical AR, reviews chart the clinical progress and persistent registration challenges of augmented reality in laparoscopic and general surgery~\cite{bernhardt2017ar,vavra2017ar}; electromagnetic navigation bronchoscopy registers at 5--10\,mm clinical error~\cite{gildea2006enb}; and SCARED~\cite{allan2021scared} and C3VD~\cite{bobrow2023c3vd} provide endoscopic data with GT depth/poses. Our work targets the registration accuracy and robustness these reviews identify as the bottleneck to clinical adoption.

\textbf{Differentiable and neural rendering.} 3D Gaussian Splatting~\cite{kerbl20233dgs} renders radiance fields in real time; EndoNeRF~\cite{wang2022endonerf} and EndoGaussian~\cite{zhu2023endogaussian} apply neural fields to deforming endoscopic scenes; nvdiffrast~\cite{laine2020nvdiffrast} provides differentiable rasterization. Our co-anchored design differs: one deformation field drives \emph{both} registration and rendering, so the renderer's photometric residual is an online validation signal rather than an independent reconstruction objective.

\section{Free-Coordinate Formulation}\label{sec:form}

\subsection{Coordinate frames and the projection chain}
Three coordinate frames are involved. The \emph{model frame} $\mathcal{F}_{m}$ holds the 3D model in its own coordinates, with points $\mathbf{X}_{m}\in\mathbb{P}^{3}$. The \emph{camera frame} $\mathcal{F}_{c}$ is the 3D frame attached to the camera, with points $\mathbf{X}_{c}\in\mathbb{P}^{3}$. The \emph{pixel frame} $\mathcal{F}_{p}$ is the 2D image frame, with points $\mathbf{x}_{p}\in\mathbb{P}^{2}$. A model point reaches the image through four maps in sequence: (i)~\emph{deformation}, $\mathbf{X}_{m}\mapsto\mathbf{X}_{m}+W(\mathbf{X}_{m})$ by a vector field $W:\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}$; (ii)~\emph{rigid pose}, the model-to-camera transform $\mathbf{T}_{cm}=(\mathbf{R},\mathbf{t})\in SE(3)$; (iii)~\emph{intrinsics}, left-multiplication by $K\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times3}$; and (iv)~\emph{projection}, the perspective division followed by optional distortion, $\pi_{D}(\mathbf{y})=D(y_{1}/y_{3},y_{2}/y_{3})$. Composing them gives the forward chain $\mathbf{x}_{p}=\pi_{D}(K\,\mathbf{T}_{cm}(\mathbf{X}_{m}+W(\mathbf{X}_{m})))$.

The pixel frame is 2D, so the inverse direction (lifting a pixel back to the camera frame) needs one extra scalar, the depth $z$: a pixel $(u,v)$ with depth $z$ back-projects to $\mathbf{X}_{c}=z\,K^{-1}[u,v,1]^{\top}$, where $z$ may come from a depth sensor, stereo disparity, a learned monocular prior, or a reconstruction. Because this inverse step scales by $z$, the \emph{quality and observability of depth is the single most important determinant of registration accuracy}: a depth error $\delta z$ maps to a camera-frame error $K^{-1}[u,v,1]^{\top}\delta z$ and hence into the recovered pose (Section~\ref{sec:rtw}). Since no frame is fixed a priori, the estimation problem is to recover the whole forward map from whatever observations are available, cross-validating the three frames against one another.

\subsection{The correspondence taxonomy}\label{sec:corr}
Estimating $F$ requires \emph{correspondences}: pairs of points known to represent the same physical location in two frames. We organize the taxonomy by the frames bridged, and give a unified solver template for each.

Each type reduces to the template \emph{correspondence} $\to$ \emph{minimal solver} $\to$ \emph{robust estimate}. For 3D--3D, given pairs $\{(\mathbf{p}_{i},\mathbf{q}_{i})\}$ the rigid alignment minimizes $\sum_{i}\|\mathbf{R}\mathbf{p}_{i}+\mathbf{t}-\mathbf{q}_{i}\|^{2}$, solved in closed form by Kabsch (or Umeyama with scale $s$). For 2D--3D, given pairs $\{(\mathbf{x}_{i},\mathbf{X}_{i})\}$ the pose solves the PnP problem, with EPnP providing an $O(n)$ minimal solution refined by RANSAC and Levenberg--Marquardt. For 2D--2D, corresponding image points constrain the essential matrix $\mathbf{E}=[\mathbf{t}]_{\times}\mathbf{R}$ via the epipolar constraint $\mathbf{x}'^{\top}\mathbf{E}\mathbf{x}=0$~\cite{longuet1981,hartley2003mvg}.

\begin{remark}[From RT to RTW]
The rigid estimate $\mathbf{T}_{cm}$ aligns the \emph{skeleton} of the model. When the target deforms, the residual after rigid alignment is precisely the deformation signal; we therefore estimate $W$ on top of the rigid skeleton (the ``RTW'' model, Section~\ref{sec:rtw}), rather than letting the rigid fit absorb deformation as pose error.
\end{remark}

Because no frame is fixed, each factor of $F$ is optional, and the pipeline degrades gracefully as priors are removed. Table~\ref{tab:degrade} summarizes which factors are required, estimable, or clamped to identity in each acquisition scenario. This degradability is the practical meaning of ``free-coordinate'': the same engine serves a calibrated RGB-D industrial scene, an uncalibrated monocular endoscope, and a deforming clinical video by clamping different factors.

\section{Method}\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Multi-source correspondence with contour arbitration}\label{sec:engine}
Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline} overviews the pipeline; this section details its correspondence front end.

\centering

\textbf{Hypothesis generation.} Single correspondence sources are individually unreliable: we measure single-source success rates of 30--60\% each, but with \emph{disjoint failure frames}. We therefore generate pose hypotheses from three complementary sources in parallel: (i)~\emph{texture} SIFT with ROI cropping and upscaling, matched and fed to EPnP+RANSAC; (ii)~\emph{geometry} FPFH+RANSAC global registration on the back-projected scene point cloud; and (iii)~\emph{appearance} render-and-compare over a multi-view template library with 24-rotation multi-start ICP refinement. Each hypothesis is refined by trimmed ICP.

\textbf{Contour-gated arbitration.} Because symmetric objects admit multiple depth-consistent poses (a $180\degree$ flip fits the depth equally well), we arbitrate among hypotheses with a composite score combining a \emph{depth-consistency} term and a \emph{contour-shape} term. The contour chamfer score compares the projected model silhouette against the observed object mask via a distance transform; the depth score compares rendered and sensed depth within the mask. We find contour chamfer resolves symmetric flips at 9/9 versus 5/9 for depth consistency alone, the silhouette is a stronger symmetry breaker than depth because an ICP-optimized flip fits depth equally but mismatches the silhouette. Runtime is 1.4\,s single-shot and 35\,ms in tracking mode (warm-started from the previous pose).

\begin{proposition}[Symmetry indistinguishability under depth]
For an object with a discrete symmetry $\mathbf{S}\in SE(3)$ mapping the visible point set to itself, the depth residual $\|\mathbf{z}_{\mathrm{render}}-\mathbf{z}_{\mathrm{sense}}\|$ is invariant under $\mathbf{T}\mapsto\mathbf{T}\mathbf{S}$ within the overlap; hence depth alone cannot disambiguate $\mathbf{T}$ from $\mathbf{T}\mathbf{S}$.
\end{proposition}
\noindent\textit{Proof:}
By definition $\mathbf{S}$ maps the visible geometry to itself, so the rendered depth image is identical for $\mathbf{T}$ and $\mathbf{T}\mathbf{S}$ wherever the two projections overlap the mask; the residual within the mask is therefore identical.

This proposition motivates contour arbitration: the silhouette \emph{does} change under a flip when the object's full (not merely visible) extent is asymmetric, breaking the degeneracy that depth cannot.

\begin{proposition}[Far-field lever amplification]\label{prop:lever}
Let the model span be $L$ about its registration center at depth $z$. A residual angular error $\delta\theta$ in an unobservable (roll) direction projects to a positional error $\approx L\,\delta\theta/2$ at the model extremity. Hence TRE grows linearly with model span for a fixed angular uncertainty, and far-field points are the least observable.
\end{proposition}
\noindent\textit{Proof:}
A rotation $\delta\theta$ about the center displaces a point at lever arm $r$ by $r\,\delta\theta$; the extremity has $r\approx L/2$. The depth residual~\eqref{eq:jacobian} weights points by their lever arm about the view axis, so distant extremities carry the largest unobservable displacement.

This lever law, confirmed empirically in the clinical cohort (Section~\ref{sec:exp}), explains why cropping the registered model to the region of interest improves TRE.

\subsection{Observability-guided and hierarchical RTW}\label{sec:rtw}

\textbf{The rigid-plus-warp model.} On top of the rigid skeleton $(\mathbf{R},\mathbf{t})$ we estimate a deformation $W$ parameterized by a free-form deformation (FFD) control lattice~\cite{sederberg1986ffd}: a regular grid of control points $\{\boldsymbol{\delta}_{j}\}$ whose displacement propagates to embedded points by trilinear interpolation,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ffd}
W(\mathbf{X}_{m}) = \sum_{j} w_{j}(\mathbf{X}_{m})\,\boldsymbol{\delta}_{j},
\end{equation}
with $w_{j}$ the trilinear basis weights. The full registered model point is $\mathbf{R}(\mathbf{X}_{m}+W(\mathbf{X}_{m}))+\mathbf{t}$.

The trilinear basis weights in~\eqref{eq:ffd} are products of one-dimensional tent functions: for a point $\mathbf{X}_{m}$ at fractional lattice coordinate $(u,v,w)$ with integer cell $(i,j,k)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:trilinear}
w_{j}(\mathbf{X}_{m}) = \prod_{a\in\{x,y,z\}}\phi_{a}\!\bigl(u_{a}-\lfloor u_{a}\rfloor\bigr),
\end{equation}
where $\phi_{a}(s)$ is $1-s$ or $s$ according to whether the control point is at the lower or upper corner along axis $a$. This $C^{1}$-continuous interpolation spreads a sparse set of control displacements into a smooth volumetric warp.

We minimize a projective data term with Huber robust kernel, regularized by magnitude, smoothness, an observability projection, and a point-to-plane normal-consistency term:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rtwobj}
\begin{aligned}
\min_{\mathbf{R},\mathbf{t},\boldsymbol{\delta}}\; & \rho\!\left(\mathrm{res}\right) + \lambda_{\mathrm{mag}}\|\boldsymbol{\delta}\|^{2} + \lambda_{\mathrm{sm}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{lap}}(\boldsymbol{\delta}) \\
& + \lambda_{\mathrm{unobs}}\bigl\|(I-\mathbf{V}_{r}\mathbf{V}_{r}^{\top})\,\mathrm{vec}(\boldsymbol{\delta})\bigr\|^{2} + \lambda_{\mathrm{p2p}}\,\rho_{\mathrm{p2p}},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $\lambda_{\mathrm{mag}},\lambda_{\mathrm{sm}},\lambda_{\mathrm{unobs}},\lambda_{\mathrm{p2p}}$ are weighting coefficients, $\rho$ is the Huber robust kernel, $\mathrm{res}$ is the depth residual of the projected model against the sensed depth (with silhouette and edge-band handling), $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{lap}}$ is a lattice Laplacian smoothness term, $\mathbf{V}_{r}$ spans the observable right-singular subspace (defined below), and the point-to-plane term $\rho_{\mathrm{p2p}}=(\mathbf{V}_{c}-\mathbf{P}_{\mathrm{obs}})\cdot\mathbf{N}_{\mathrm{obs}}$ anchors tangential drift; here $\mathbf{V}_{c}$ is the deformed model point in the camera frame, $\mathbf{P}_{\mathrm{obs}}$ the matched observed surface point, and $\mathbf{N}_{\mathrm{obs}}$ its surface normal.

\textbf{Observability of the depth-residual operator.} Let $\mathbf{r}\in\mathbb{R}^{M}$ be the stacked depth residual over $M$ observed model points, and let $\boldsymbol{\theta}$ collect the optimization variables (pose increments and lattice displacements). The Jacobian of the residual is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:jacobian}
\mathbf{J} = \frac{\partial\mathbf{r}}{\partial\boldsymbol{\theta}} = -\,\mathbf{W}\otimes\mathbf{R}\,\mathbf{z},
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{W}$ is the FFD basis-weight matrix collecting the $w_{j}$ of~\eqref{eq:ffd}, $\mathbf{R}$ the rotation, $\mathbf{z}$ the stacked depths of the observed model points, and $\otimes$ the Kronecker product, so $\mathbf{J}$ is a sparse block operator. Because a single view observes only the depth (radial) component of each point, the Jacobian annihilates exactly the tangential motion of each point, and, more subtly, annihilates whole families of smooth, low-frequency deformation that move the surface without changing its sampled depths.

\begin{theorem}[Rank deficiency of single-view registration]\label{thm:rank}
The singular spectrum of $\mathbf{J}$ for single-view projective registration of a smooth surface decays to numerical threshold within a minority of directions: in our canonical configuration, only 151 of 648 singular values exceed $10^{-3}\sigma_{\max}$, where $\sigma_{\max}$ is the largest singular value (23\%). The null space includes (i)~\emph{tubular roll} (rotation of a cylinder about its axis); (ii)~\emph{tangential flow} (motion of points within the visible surface); and (iii)~\emph{smooth low-frequency warps} that preserve the sampled depth profile.
\end{theorem}
The proof is by direct singular-value decomposition of the assembled $\mathbf{J}$~\eqref{eq:jacobian}; we report the empirical spectrum rather than an analytic bound because it depends on geometry and sampling.

\begin{definition}[Observable subspace]
Let $\mathbf{J}=\mathbf{U}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}\mathbf{V}^{\top}$ be the SVD. The \emph{observable subspace} is $\mathrm{span}(\mathbf{V}_{r})$, the right-singular vectors with $\sigma>\sigma_{\max}\cdot10^{-3}$; its orthogonal complement is the \emph{unobservable subspace}.
\end{definition}

The spectral projection $\mathbf{V}_{r}\mathbf{V}_{r}^{\top}$ appears in~\eqref{eq:rtwobj} to penalize motion in the unobservable subspace, so the optimizer deforms only where the image can constrain it. This both stabilizes the optimization and prevents the deformation field from hallucinating unobserved structure. A concrete failure of \emph{un}guided optimization illustrates the point: a determinant-corrupting rotation bug ($\det\mathbf{R}\approx49$) made the depth residual converge to 0.002 while shape error reached 268\%---the model slid along view rays to fit depth while its geometry diverged. This ``ray-sliding'' is precisely motion in the unobservable subspace of Theorem~\ref{thm:rank}: the residual cannot see it, so the optimizer corrupts geometry while reporting success (Section~\ref{sec:disc}).

\textbf{Hierarchical and region-focused refinement.} Global smooth warps and sharp local features live at different frequencies; we run a hierarchical wrapper (coarse $4^{3}$, then $8^{3}$, then $12^{3}$ lattice), focusing each finer level on the top-percentile high-residual region with consistently scaled intrinsics, giving the local feature denser observation support. This addresses the fundamental \emph{edge-residual competition}: pose error creates large residuals at the silhouette edge that drown a small local dent's signal in the global loss.

\subsection{Manifold temporal consistency with innovation gating}\label{sec:kalman}

\textbf{Manifold filter with right-invariant innovation.} We track the pose $\mathbf{T}_{t}\in SE(3)$ and a manifold velocity $\boldsymbol{\nu}_{t}\in\mathfrak{se}(3)$, with constant-velocity prediction on the manifold $\hat{\mathbf{T}}_{t}^{-}=\mathbf{T}_{t-1}\exp(\boldsymbol{\nu}_{t-1}^{\wedge}\Delta t)$, where $\exp:\mathfrak{se}(3)\to SE(3)$ and $\wedge$ maps the twist to its Lie-algebra element~\cite{sola2018lie}. A measurement $\mathbf{T}_{t}^{\mathrm{meas}}$ (from the engine) is compared to the prediction by the \emph{right-invariant} innovation $\boldsymbol{\eta}_{t}=\log((\hat{\mathbf{T}}_{t}^{-})^{-1}\mathbf{T}_{t}^{\mathrm{meas}})^{\vee}\in\mathbb{R}^{6}$, expressed in the body frame and equivariant to the trajectory, a left-invariant innovation would mix frames and corrupt the covariance update. We separate translation and rotation noise scales, since a single process-noise magnitude is physically meaningless across units (millimetres versus radians).

\textbf{Innovation gating.} The state covariance $\mathbf{P}$ over the augmented state $[\boldsymbol{\xi},\boldsymbol{\nu}]$ propagates as $\mathbf{P}^{-}=\mathbf{F}\mathbf{P}\mathbf{F}^{\top}+\mathbf{Q}$, where $\mathbf{F}$ is the manifold transition Jacobian (identity on the pose block, $\Delta t$ coupling into velocity) and $\mathbf{Q}$ is the process noise with separated translation/rotation and velocity blocks. On a gated-accepted measurement the update is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kalmanupdate}
\mathbf{K}_{g}=\mathbf{P}^{-}\mathbf{H}^{\top}(\mathbf{H}\mathbf{P}^{-}\mathbf{H}^{\top}+\mathbf{R})^{-1},\quad
\mathbf{P}=(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{K}_{g}\mathbf{H})\mathbf{P}^{-},
\end{equation}
with $\mathbf{R}$ the measurement covariance and $\mathbf{H}$ the innovation Jacobian; the pose increment $\mathbf{K}_{g}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{t}$ is retracted onto the manifold by the exponential. Real engines occasionally return catastrophic poses (symmetric flips, tracking loss). Feeding these into the filter resets the trajectory. We therefore gate on the normalized innovation magnitude $d_{t}=\boldsymbol{\eta}_{t}^{\top}(\mathbf{H}\mathbf{P}^{-}\mathbf{H}^{\top}+\mathbf{R})^{-1}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{t}$: if $d_{t}$ exceeds a threshold, the measurement is rejected and the filter \emph{coasts} on its prediction; a full \emph{relocation} (state reset) is triggered only after $N$ consecutive rejections. This converts fat-tailed engine failures into short coasts rather than trajectory resets, and is the difference between 34--60\% jitter reduction and the catastrophic drift of an ungated filter. We analyze the accuracy--smoothness trade by a paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test on the per-frame TRE.

\subsection{Co-anchored registration, rendering, and scene graph}\label{sec:render}

\textbf{Co-anchored registration and differentiable rendering.} One FFD anchor field $W$ parameterizes \emph{both} the RTW warp~\eqref{eq:ffd} and the deformation of a 3D Gaussian Splatting model: the Gaussian means are warped by $W$ while their covariances are frozen under the small-deformation assumption. This co-anchoring is the key design: the renderer's photometric residual is then an \emph{online, label-free validation signal} for the registration, because a misregistered or mis-deformed model renders with photometric error. We implement a vectorized, differentiable EWA splatter~\cite{kerbl20233dgs} with exact front-to-back alpha compositing, $\hat{\mathbf{I}}=\sum_{k}T_{k}\alpha_{k}\mathbf{c}_{k}$ with $T_{k}=\prod_{j<k}(1-\alpha_{j})$ the transmittance, computed by a segmented float64 cumulative sum of $\log(1-\alpha)$ for numerical stability. The renderer reaches 47.4\,dB PSNR at 142\,FPS at object scale.

\textbf{Scene graph and endoscopic centerline.} For multi-model scenes we maintain a scene graph whose nodes are registered models and whose edges encode occlusion relations; depth-aware compositing resolves which model is in front at each pixel, achieving 100\% occlusion-edge accuracy in a triple-occlusion test scene. For monocular endoscopy, where texture and depth are unreliable, we register a 3D skeletonized CT airway centerline to the 2D skeleton of the frame's lumen-dark region by minimizing the distance-transform chamfer of the projected centerline, with junction weighting and view-frustum cropping. This structural correspondence registers $0.0\degree$/6.6\,mm inside the airway where depth ICP collapses (38.1\,mm), providing the viable path when depth is unavailable.

\textbf{Learned front-end with geometric refinement (hybrid cascade).} The multi-source correspondence engine of Section~\ref{sec:engine} is accurate but costs 1.4\,s per single-shot. We therefore \emph{amortize} it with a learned front-end, \textbf{FreeRegNet}, that regresses the free-coordinate map directly and is refined by geometric ICP, a hybrid that keeps the geometric engine's accuracy while approaching feed-forward speed. FreeRegNet is the free-coordinate objective made learnable: a lightweight vision-transformer backbone (timm ViT-S/16, or a Qwen2-VL vision tower with VLoRA) encodes the object ROI, fused with a PointNet-style encoding of the canonical model points (object conditioning), and a pose head outputs a 6D rotation and a depth. Following the ROI-decoupling principle, the full translation is recovered analytically from the predicted depth and the observed image position (the crop center) via $\mathbf{t}=z\,K^{-1}[c_{x},c_{y},1]^{\top}$, so the network learns only appearance-dependent quantities (rotation, depth) while geometry supplies the rest.

Training minimizes the free-coordinate objective end-to-end (a geodesic rotation loss---replaced by a differentiable ADD-S point-cloud loss for symmetric objects---plus translation and depth-consistency terms) over all 13 objects with crop/illumination augmentation and cosine annealing. At inference the network's pose seeds a trimmed ICP against the depth back-projection. This learned front-end is used \emph{only} for rigid-object pose; the clinical path remains the structural centerline method, because general learned pose front-ends fail out-of-distribution in endoscopy (Section~\ref{sec:exp}).

We also tested whether a large vision--language model helps: substituting a Qwen2-VL-2B vision tower (with VLoRA) for the lightweight ViT gives no accuracy gain at equal budget at $\sim$100$\times$ the parameters and $\sim$6$\times$ the per-epoch cost. A general VLM's language-aligned features do not specifically benefit geometric pose regression, so we retain the lightweight ViT backbone and report this as a negative result.

The complete FreeReg pipeline ties these components into a single per-frame procedure. A one-time preparation skeletonizes the model to its centerline, builds the FFD control lattice, and initializes surface Gaussians. Each frame then proceeds in six stages: (1)~\emph{correspondence}, generating pose hypotheses from SIFT, FPFH, and render-and-compare; (2)~\emph{arbitration}, scoring candidates by contour and depth to select a pose; (3)~\emph{gating}, applying the right-invariant innovation test to reject outliers, coast, or relocate; (4)~\emph{rigid fusion} of the gated measurement with the filter prediction; (5)~\emph{warp}, running observability-guided RTW~\eqref{eq:rtwobj} within the observable subspace with hierarchical focusing; and (6)~\emph{render}, compositing the co-anchored 3DGS model whose photometric residual feeds back as validation. The output per frame is the pose $\mathbf{T}_{t}$, the deformation $W$, and the fused render.

\section{Experiments and Results}\label{sec:exp}


\textbf{Implementation.} The pipeline is implemented in PyTorch 2.8 (CUDA 12.8) with OpenCV, Open3D, and NumPy, on an NVIDIA RTX GPU. All geometric operations (SE(3)/SO(3) exp/log, projection, rasterization) are differentiable where gradients are needed. All evaluation scripts, the Windows build patches for FoundationPose and Endo3R, and a gallery of result figures across every dataset are released.

\textbf{Metrics.} We report ADD/ADD-S and the BOP protocol (MSSD/MSPD/VSD, self-implemented) for single-frame pose; reprojection RMSE; pixel-jitter RMS and deviation for temporal consistency; target registration error (TRE, nearest-neighbour) for clinical registration; deformation-recovery error as a fraction of model extent; silhouette IoU and photometric residual for AR fusion fidelity; and PSNR/SSIM and FPS for rendering. Statistical comparison uses the Wilcoxon signed-rank test.

\textbf{Datasets.} We validate one and the same free-coordinate engine on six resources spanning rigid, deformable, and clinical domains, summarized in Table~\ref{tab:datasets}; each provides ground truth for quantitative metrics, and we compare against SOTA where applicable. We explicitly separate two evidence classes throughout: (a)~\emph{real-CT geometry with synthetic fly-through}, which provides ground-truth poses for quantitative TRE; and (b)~\emph{real intra-operative bronchoscopic video}, which lacks ground truth and is used only for qualitative AR demonstration (Section~\ref{sec:exp}); the quantitative clinical numbers derive from class (a). (iii)~\emph{SCARED}~\cite{allan2021scared}: 197 frames of one real endoscopic keyframe sequence with a GT camera trajectory, for real-video temporal and AR evaluation. (iv)~\emph{Virtual fly-through}: 200 frames of a synthetic trajectory over real CAD geometry for controlled temporal evaluation. (v)~\emph{BOP Linemod}~\cite{hodan2018bop}: 125 test samples (5 scenes $\times$ 25 frames, with RGB-D, masks, and GT 6D poses) for single-frame rigid registration against the pose literature; the learned front-end (FreeRegNet) trains on the full 13 objects with an 85/15 train/validation split per object. (vi)~\emph{BOP T-LESS}~\cite{hodan2018bop}: 315 test samples of texture-less, symmetric objects, evaluated with the official ADD-S metric. All datasets are used for testing only; only FreeRegNet trains, on BOP objects as noted. SOTA comparisons use the official FoundationPose and Endo3R implementations and open3d ICP baselines. Additional public resources---EndoNeRF, C3VD colon meshes, Stereo\_Lap, BlendedMVS, DTU, and the full 20-scene T-LESS---are available for extension but are not required for the present evidence chain.

\subsection{Non-rigid registration and observability}
Table~\ref{tab:nonrigid} reports RTW results on the real-colon FEM benchmark and the synthetic observability analysis. The observability rank of the single-view depth-residual operator is 151/648 (23\%), confirming Theorem~\ref{thm:rank}: most deformation directions are unobservable from one view. Figure~\ref{fig:dent} visualizes this on real-colon frames (RTW reproduces the deformation the rigid fit cannot express); its right panel shows recovery degrading monotonically as deformation rotates into the unobservable subspace---a direct measurement of Theorem~\ref{thm:rank}.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:disc}

\textbf{Toward general-purpose, calibration-free AR/MR.} FreeReg registers an arbitrary model to an arbitrary stream without calibrated intrinsics or external tracking, and one engine validates across rigid, deformable, and clinical domains. In the clinical proving ground it registers the CT airway at 4.23\,mm mean error (92\% under 10\,mm) at 30--80\,FPS---matching the 5--10\,mm accuracy of electromagnetic navigation without a field generator. The far-field lever law implies that cropping the model to the region of interest roughly triples accuracy (2.9\,mm versus 8.0\,mm, $r=0.81$) at no hardware cost. The observability analysis tells the operator in advance how much deformation the view supports (23\% of directions), flagging unreliable estimates rather than silently drifting.

\textbf{Observability is the central scarcity.} Across experiments the same theme recurs: tubular roll, smooth-warp rigidity equivalence, tangential deformation, far-field lever amplification, and ray-sliding. The observability decomposition unifies these: it explains why metrics must decompose axial and lateral error, why single-view full-3D warp recovery is ill-posed, and why recovery ceilings are observational, not algorithmic, limits.

\textbf{Observability is a property of the sensing, not the model.} The 23\% rank is the \emph{single-view} bound, not fixed: a second view raises it to 41\% (44\% at six views, wider baselines helping more), and the recoverable deformation energy rises from 39\% to 59\%. Since FreeReg accepts arbitrary inputs, stereo or multi-view acquisition enlarges the recoverable subspace---a tunable hardware-level upgrade when a task needs more than the monocular 23\%.

\textbf{Limitations and future work.} Our evidence has clear boundaries. \emph{Generality}: arbitrary model-to-stream registration is the design goal, not a fully established claim; we validate on rigid CAD/BOP, CT airways, and endoscopic video. \emph{Clinical evidence}: quantitative TRE uses real CT with synthetic fly-throughs; real intra-operative video is qualitative, and multi-center cohorts are needed. \emph{Benchmark relation}: the BOP comparison is a cross-domain sanity check (FoundationPose targets rigid calibrated pose, FreeReg calibration-free deforming registration). \emph{Deployment}: the loop must run inside the endoscopy hardware budget, feasible per our 30--80\,FPS but unvalidated in the operating room.

\textbf{Reproducibility.} The full FreeReg pipeline (geometry kernels, correspondence engine, observability-guided RTW, manifold Kalman filter, co-anchored renderer and scene graph, FreeRegNet front-end, evaluation scripts) and the Windows build patches for FoundationPose/Endo3R are released at \url{https://github.com/liranyang123456-commits/FreeReg}. Clinical data are subject to the hospital's data-governance process (Ethics Statement).

% CONCLUSION
%==============================================================================
\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
We presented FreeReg, a free-coordinate framework that registers an arbitrary 3D model onto an arbitrary image or video stream for augmented and mixed reality, without a privileged world frame or calibrated intrinsics. By estimating the free-coordinate projection and constraining deformation to the provably observable subspace of the single-view operator, one engine matches or exceeds SOTA on rigid benchmarks (BOP Linemod AR 0.848, T-LESS ADD-S 0.921), recovers deformation on real tissue (1.1\% of extent on real-colon), and stays correct where general-purpose methods collapse in clinical endoscopy (FoundationPose TRE 104\,mm, Endo3R ATE 34\,mm, versus FreeReg 4.23\,mm mean error at 30--80\,FPS---electromagnetic-navigation accuracy without external tracking hardware). The observability analysis quantifies which deformation directions a sensing condition can and cannot recover, giving AR/MR deployment an explicit reliability budget---and, because observability is a property of the sensing rather than the model, a second view nearly doubles the recoverable subspace (23\%$\to$41\% rank), a concrete hardware-level upgrade path. Remaining steps are prospective, multi-center validation on real bronchoscopic video and broader cross-domain benchmarks.
